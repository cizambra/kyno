A direction change during a run

Follow one customer complaint through planning, drafting, a direction update, and a revised reply. Each step shows who acts, what happens, and the result.

Task

Draft a reply to the delivery complaint and recommend next steps.

Allowed actions: The agent may propose a refund of up to $40 and human investigation. It can draft a reply; it cannot execute either action.

Application · step 1

Receives the customer’s request

Incoming support ticket and supplied order facts. The ticket is an illustration of the recorded scenario.

Customer request
“I paid $40 for express delivery, but my package arrived two days late. This is the second late express delivery this month. Please refund the fee and explain what happened.”

Order context
$40 express fee; both late deliveries confirmed; causes unknown.

Result

The task and customer context are ready for planning.

No plan or draft yet.

- Be accurate
  Explain what is known and never claim an action was completed when it was only proposed.

Agent · step 3

Creates a response plan

Receives direction v1, the task, and the same customer context.

1. Draft a response: Plan to acknowledge the confirmed two-day delay and the earlier late express delivery, propose refunding this delivery’s $40 fee, and explain that the causes are unknown. Propose escalation to a person to investigate the repeated delays without making the straightforward refund depend on that investigation. This balances prompt, fair resolution with attention to the recurring problem.
2. Finalize it: Check that the reply states the proposed refund and investigation clearly, requests no information already confirmed, and makes no claim that either action has happened. This follows the principles of making remedies easy and being accurate while avoiding unnecessary back-and-forth.

Result

Agent · step 4

Creates the first draft

Receives freshly pulled direction v1, the customer context, and its saved plan.

I'm sorry your express delivery arrived two days late, especially after another late express delivery earlier this month. Tracking confirms this delivery's delay, and your order history confirms the earlier delay.

I propose refunding the full $40 express delivery fee for this delivery. I also recommend escalating the repeated delays to a person for investigation. We don't yet know what caused either delay, so I can't give you a confirmed explanation. The proposed refund should proceed independently of that investigation. Neither the refund nor the escalation has been carried out.

Operator · step 5

Updates mission and principles

The workflow pauses after the first draft. The operator commits new direction in Kyno.

Mission

Resolve delivery complaints fairly while reducing recurring delivery failures.

Principles

  • Address immediate harm
    Make proportionate remedies straightforward when a paid delivery promise was missed.
  • Look beyond individual complaints
    Use recurring complaints to identify problems that may affect other customers. Choose proportionate follow-up without delaying an appropriate individual remedy.
  • Be accurate
    Explain what is known and never claim an action was completed when it was only proposed.

Result

Kyno now stores v2. The existing plan and draft stay intact.

Direction in Kyno: v2. Saved plan: v1. Draft retained.

Application / LangGraph · step 6

Checks whether the plan is current

The current direction is v2.

v2 is newer than v1, so this workflow selects its replanning branch.

Result

The application chooses to revise the remaining work. Kyno supplies direction; it does not choose the workflow branch.

Current direction: v2. Saved plan: v1. Replanning selected.

About this recording

The first draft already proposes a refund and an investigation. After the direction update, the remaining plan expands the investigation to possible shared delivery problems and ways to reduce recurrence. Refund and investigation remain compatible throughout.

This is a real Kyno and LangGraph run using Codex agents through a temporary bridge. The customer scenario is fictional, and the ticket view illustrates input supplied as text. No customer messages, refunds, or escalations were executed. Each model call used a fresh direction pull. This single run illustrates delivery and interpretation; later calls also receive the existing plan and draft.
